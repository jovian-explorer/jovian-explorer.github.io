Solar wind · Radio occultation · Planetary plasma

Keshav Aggarwal

Student visitor, Space Physics Laboratory, Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre, Thiruvananthapuram.

I am a space-science researcher studying the solar wind and its interaction with planetary bodies. My work uses spacecraft radio signals as probes of the solar corona, interplanetary medium, and planetary ionospheres. As a radio signal travels through these regions, fluctuations in electron density along its path imprint themselves on the signal’s frequency and amplitude. By analysing these fluctuations, I infer solar-wind speeds, coronal electron densities, and parameters describing plasma turbulence.

My research draws on observations from the Indian Mars Orbiter Mission, Chandrayaan-2, Chandrayaan-3, Akatsuki, and Venus Express. I submitted my Ph.D. thesis, Exploring Space Weather and Its Effect on Planetary Atmospheres Using Radio Occultation, in May 2026 under the supervision of Prof. Abhirup Datta. This work was supported by the Prime Minister’s Research Fellowship (PMRF), which I was awarded in October 2023.

During my M.Sc., I joined the JWST Transiting Exoplanet Community Early Release Science team and contributed to a paper on the first identification of carbon dioxide in an exoplanet atmosphere. Alongside research, I write about astronomy on Medium, create videos on YouTube, and worked on public-outreach activities at DAASE IIT Indore. I am broadly interested in the Sun–planet connection, planetary atmospheres, space-weather effects, radio astronomy, and the use of spacecraft observations to explore the Solar System.
